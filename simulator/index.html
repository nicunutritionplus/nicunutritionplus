Virtual cohort

2,000 simulated infants run through the plan set above. Each infant has its own birth weight and GA; MOM availability (21.5% none, 36.5% partial, 42% full, as in your EPS cohort of 107 infants at 500–1000 g); MOM and donor-pool composition; fat loss of 5–15%; and daily feeding events (feeds held on 6% of days below 750 g and 3% at 750–1000 g, no advance on 10% of days). The MOM share comes from each infant's own supply, not from the simulator's milk mix. The fortifiers and fortified shares do come from the simulator.

Model assumptions and sources

  • Requirements. DOL 1: energy ≥45 kcal/kg/d and amino acids ≥1.5 g/kg/d; DOL 2–6: protein ≥2.5 g/kg/d with non-protein energy >65 kcal/kg/d (ESPGHAN/ESPEN/ESPR/CSPEN 2018 pediatric PN guidelines: van Goudoever et al. and Joosten et al., Clin Nutr 2018;37:2309–2323). DOL 7 onward: energy ≥115 kcal/kg/d and protein ≥3.5 g/kg/d, about 4.0 for extremely preterm infants, ceiling 4.5, P:E 2.8–3.6 g/100 kcal (Embleton et al., ESPGHAN 2022, JPGN 2023;76:248–268). Enteral and PN intakes are summed as delivered.
  • Mother's own milk. True protein and energy by lactation day from Gidrewicz & Fenton, BMC Pediatr 2014 (days 1–3: 2.7 g/dL, 49 kcal/dL; days 4–7: 1.7, 65; week 2: 1.5, 70; weeks 3–4: 1.4, 68), interpolated by day. Colostrum protein includes secretory IgA and lactoferrin, which are only partly digested.
  • MOM supply and milk mix. The MOM share is a share of milk volume. With "Ramps up", MOM supply follows a logistic curve (half of a 400 mL/d maximum by day 4.5) and donor milk fills the gap. For a 750 g infant on 60/+30, that caps MOM at about 11% of the milk on DOL 1, 25% on DOL 2 and 59% on DOL 3; from DOL 4 supply covers any share. In the cohort, provider type, maximum supply and timing vary by infant.
  • Unfortified milk. The unfortified part of each milk is fed plain, so the fortifier dose per mL of milk falls in proportion. For intake, fortifying 80% of the milk at full strength equals fortifying all of it at 80% strength. Choosing Unfortified as a milk's fortifier feeds all of that milk plain. A protein module goes only into fortified feeds.
  • Donor milk. 0.9 g/dL protein and 65 kcal/dL by default (ESPGHAN 2022: "around 0.9–1.0 g/dL or less"); cohort pools vary (SD 0.12 g/dL and 6 kcal/dL) and change every 4 days.
  • Fortifiers (label values; liquid products displace milk). Enfamil Liquid HMF High Protein: 7.5 kcal, 0.56 g protein per 5 mL, 5 mL per 25 mL milk (label). Enfamil Liquid HMF Standard Protein: 7.5 kcal, 0.42 g per 5 mL, same ratio (Canadian label; consistent with the US label's 3.4 g per 100 kcal). With a MOM fortifier set, each milk is fortified with its own product before the two are combined. Similac HMF Extensively Hydrolyzed Protein Concentrated Liquid: 7 kcal, 0.5 g per 5 mL, same ratio (Abbott). Prolact+4 and +6: 29.4 kcal and about 1.22 g protein per 20 mL, mixed 20:80 and 30:70 (derived from Prolacta's nutrition brochure). Prolact RTF 24 and RTF 26 are ready-to-feed products made from donor milk: 85 and 92 kcal and 2.4 and 2.7 g protein per 100 mL as fed (same brochure, Rev 5, 09/25). They are fed in place of donor milk at those label values, in the virtual cohort too, where lot-to-lot variation is not modeled. MOM given alongside gets Prolact+4 or +6 unless another MOM fortifier is chosen. The standard powder is generic, adding 1.1 g protein and 15 kcal per 100 mL.
  • PN. Amino acids 4 kcal/g, dextrose 3.4 kcal/g, 20% lipid 2.0 kcal/mL (5 mL per g, counted inside the PN volume). Amino acids are counted 1:1 as protein. Other IV fluids (arterial line, medications, flushes) are not counted as nutrition or fluid.
  • Losses. Fat lost to tubing and syringes, 10% by default (intermittent gavage), converted to energy at 50% of feed energy from fat. Continuous infusion can lose 20–30%.
  • Feeding events. Held days and no-advance days in the cohort are calibrated to your EPS cohort (NPO on 3% of post-first-feed days at 750–1000 g, about 6% at 25–29 weeks). After a held day, feeds resume at the last tolerated volume. Doses are per kg of birth weight; postnatal weight loss (median nadir 16% in EPS) would raise intake per kg of current weight.
  • Not modeled. Glucose homeostasis, minerals and sodium, osmolality, NEC or sepsis risk, residual-based feeding practices, and absorption differences between enteral and parenteral nutrients.
